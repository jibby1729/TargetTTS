%% SLIDE 1: Problem & Motivation
\begin{frame}
\frametitle{The Problem: ASR Fails on Overlapping Speech}

\small
\begin{itemize}
    \item Modern ASR systems like Whisper perform well on clean, single-speaker audio
    \item But in meetings, courtrooms, and media, speakers frequently overlap
    \item Standard ASR has no notion of which speaker to transcribe and it tries to transcribe everything, which causes errors.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

Goal: Given a short enrollment clip of a target speaker and a multi-speaker mixture, transcribe only the target speaker.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Why does this matter?}
\small
Accurate per-speaker transcription is needed in courtrooms, podcasts, debates, and meetings where speakers frequently talk over each other. It can also support full-duplex speech models that need labelled transcriptions of natural conversations with interruptions.
\end{block}

\end{frame}


%% SLIDE 2: Whisper architecture + fine-tuning
\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Our ASR Model: Whisper}

\small

We use OpenAI's Whisper as our ASR backbone. Whisper is a transformer-based encoder-decoder model trained on 680{,}000 hours of multilingual speech.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\centering
\includegraphics[height=0.42\textheight]{whispermodel.png}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\flushleft
\small
\begin{itemize}
    \item English: We use Whisper-tiny (39M params) off the shelf
    \item Twi (Akan): Whisper does not support Twi natively, so we fine-tuned Whisper-small (244M params) on the WAXAL Akan dataset
    \item Both models serve as baselines to measure how overlap degrades transcription and whether the trend is observed across languages
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

%% SLIDE 4: Our approach + what's next
\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Proposed Solution and Next Steps}

\small
Our approach: add a speaker-conditioned extraction stage before ASR. Whisper stays frozen.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Extract a speaker embedding from a short enrollment clip using a pretrained speaker verification model
    \item A lightweight trained module uses the embedding to produce a time-frequency mask that isolates the target speaker from the mixture
    \item Whisper (kept fixed) transcribes the cleaned signal
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Remaining Work}
\small
\begin{itemize}
    \item Implement speaker embedding extraction and conditioned masking module
    \item Train on synthetic mixtures, evaluate WER improvement over the unconditioned baseline
    \item Ablations: enrollment clip length, non-target leakage, cross-lingual transfer
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\end{frame}
